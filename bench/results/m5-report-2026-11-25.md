# M5 report, 2026-11-25

[English](m5-report-2026-11-25.md) | [简体中文](m5-report-2026-11-25.zh-CN.md)

The end of M5 (caching and extensions, I67–I75) for akit, as fit's
[M5 report](https://github.com/lesismal/fit/blob/3658ae9/bench/results/m5-report-2026-10-23.md) is
for fit: the roadmap's three exit criteria checked one by one, the full harness with the cache
scenarios, and *Where akit stands* (rule 28). akit is I76's working tree on `346ad8b` (I76's
commit: its harness changes, a test, and a converter function split in two, none on the data
path), on akio `dad77a7a`; fit at `7daee573` (`bench/FIT_REV`).

M5's exit criteria hold:

- **The cache against nginx's `proxy_cache`**: on the harness's cache scenarios akit keeps what
  nginx keeps, and a little more (99.98% of `cache-zipf`'s requests answered from the cache,
  nginx's 99.86%, fit's 99.97%), at 1.5 times nginx's rate over HTTP/1.1, 1.75 times for 100,000
  paths and 6.2 times over h2c, first of the three. A stampede of 1,000 clients on a cold key makes
  one fetch, over HTTP/1.1 and h2c, as nginx's and fit's do (`bench/stampede.sh`, new).
- **A WASM plugin that edits headers**: the same `.wasm`, fit's own test plugin, runs in fit and
  akit; it adds 14.7 µs of CPU a request in akit (6.8 µs in fit), bounded by its pool's instances,
  queue, call timeout and memory, and past the queue answers 503 at once. akit's cost is twice
  fit's: akio-wasm interprets where wazero compiles (akio API request A180, akio's W5).
- **The nginx converter**: fit's sample configs, and the other nginx configs akit's tests convert,
  all convert, and every directive left out is listed at its line (a probe directive in each of
  their 56 blocks, new); the output and warnings are fit's byte for byte (I74).

Also in this report: the full harness with every server, where akit is first in fixed, static over
HTTP/1.1 and the cache, third in the proxy, whose memory grows by akio's A171 (found by I25); and
FastCGI's PHP-FPM test, which I75 could not run, passing against PHP-FPM 8.4.

## The cache against nginx's `proxy_cache`

The harness's two cache scenarios (fit's I69), with akit added in I76 as fit and nginx are
configured: a 1 GiB cache with its lock on, in front of fitbench's cache origin (nginx answering
every path with 4 KiB fresh for 10 minutes, on the backends' CPUs), whose `stub_status` counts what
reaches it during the measured 30s, from which the hit ratio comes. `cache-hot` asks for one path
again and again; `cache-zipf` for 100,000 paths, the n-th most popular as often as 1/n, over
HTTP/1.1 only.

| Scenario | Protocol | nginx | fit | akit | akit / nginx | Hits: nginx | fit | akit |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `cache-hot` | HTTP/1.1 | 398,691 | 526,977 | **589,973** | 1.48 | 100.00% | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| `cache-hot` | h2c | 337,106 | 1,694,120 | **2,086,275** | 6.19 | 100.00% | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| `cache-zipf` | HTTP/1.1 | 322,575 | 505,880 | **564,220** | 1.75 | 99.86% | 99.97% | 99.98% |

Requests a second. In `cache-zipf` the origin answered 13,590 of nginx's 9.7 million requests, 4,037
of fit's 15.2 million and 3,452 of akit's 17.0 million: all three hold every path the scenario asks
for, and what still reaches the origin is the paths first asked for during the measured run; fewer
for fit and akit, whose locks make one fetch of a path several clients ask for at once.

akit keeps what nginx's `proxy_cache` keeps, and more of it, at 1.5 times nginx's rate over HTTP/1.1
for one path and 1.75 times for 100,000, and 6.2 times over h2c; ahead of fit by 12% to 23%.
Its CPU a request is the lowest of the three in each (84,522, 298,957 and 80,835 requests a
CPU-second, fit's 75,748, 243,750 and 72,468). In `cache-zipf` akit's peak memory is 519 MiB for
about 430 MB of responses kept in its own memory (fit 1,056 MiB, its Go heap's headroom on top;
nginx 214 MiB, its responses on disk in the page cache, which anonymous memory does not count), part
of it the proxy's growth below.

### A stampede: one fetch

[`bench/stampede.sh`](../stampede.sh) (new in I76): 1,000 clients ask for one cold key at once,
while the origin takes a second to answer and counts what reaches it. Over HTTP/1.1 each client is
a connection of its own; over h2c the 1,000 requests are streams, 100 on each of 10 connections.
Three cold keys a server and protocol, each server alone on the server's CPUs. Results:
[`m5-stampede-2026-11-25`](m5-stampede-2026-11-25/results.txt).

| Server | Cache lock | HTTP/1.1: answered 200 | origin fetches | h2c: answered 200 | origin fetches |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| nginx | `proxy_cache_lock on` | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 |
| fit | on (its default) | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 |
| akit | on (its default) | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1, 1, 1 |
| akit | `disable_lock` (the control) | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 1,000, 1,000, 1,000 | 829, 829, 829 |

akit makes one fetch for a thousand clients, as nginx and fit do, over both protocols; every client
is answered from that fetch. The control shows the check can see a stampede: without the lock every
HTTP/1.1 client's miss reaches the origin, and over h2c 829 of the 1,000 do, the same 829 for each
key (why not all 1,000 was not looked into; the control only has to show that a stampede, when
there is one, is seen). The tests hold the same at a smaller scale on every run: fit's
`TestStampede`, ported in I68 (100 clients, HTTP/1.1 and h2c, and through the proxy with a
streamed body), in `crates/akit-cache/tests/cache.rs`.

## A WASM plugin that edits headers

The same `.wasm` in fit and in akit: fit's own test plugin, `plugin/wasm/testdata/headers` (fit's
ABI, a Go `wasip1` guest; akit's `crates/akit-plugin/tests/guests/headers-go.wasm.gz`, byte for
byte what akio-wasm's tests run). For the scenario's path it sets one request field and removes
another, and adds three response fields. The `wasm` scenario (new in I76) is the fixed response
behind the `wasm` middleware with its defaults in both, an instance a CPU (7), a queue of 1,024,
100 ms a call; `fixed`, measured in the same container, is the baseline. The servers `akit-wasm`
and `fit-wasm` are akit and fit with their harness configs and one more config file each, adding
the route ([`bench/README.md`](../README.md#the-wasm-plugins-cost)); fit's own config is unchanged.
Results: [`m5-wasm-2026-11-25`](m5-wasm-2026-11-25/report.md).

| Server | Protocol | fixed | wasm | wasm / fixed | CPU a request: fixed | wasm | The plugin's cost |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| akit | HTTP/1.1 | 831,888 | 312,679 | 38% | 7.52 µs | 22.25 µs | **14.7 µs** |
| fit | HTTP/1.1 | 785,657 | 452,353 | 58% | 8.15 µs | 14.92 µs | **6.8 µs** |

Requests a second, 256 connections; CPU a request is the server's CPU over its requests. The p99
with the plugin: akit 2.50 ms, fit 2.45 ms (0.32 and 2.76 ms without it). Peak memory with the
plugin: akit 81 MiB (48 without: 7 instances of a Go guest), fit 235 MiB (123).

**Bounded.** Over h2c, 64 connections of 32 streams put 2,048 requests in flight against 7
instances and a queue of 1,024: both servers answer what does not fit at once, with 5xx (the 503
of a full queue), 95% of akit's requests and 94% of fit's, and serve the rest, 105,611 and 137,338
a second, with a mean of 0.98 and 0.85 ms and at most 23 and 14 ms. The memory stays where it was
(akit 92 MiB, fit 244 MiB). Past the queue nothing waits; a call is bounded by its 100 ms timeout,
an instance's memory by `max_memory_bytes` (128 MiB), a wait for an instance by `wait_timeout`
(1 s), each tested since I71 (fit's `TestFailures`, `TestNeverBlocks`, `TestParams`, in
`crates/akit-plugin/tests/wasm.rs`, on epoll and io_uring); calls never run on an I/O thread.

akit's plugin costs about twice fit's, a request (14.7 µs against 6.8): fit runs the guest on
wazero's compiler, akit on akio-wasm's interpreter, which has no `unsafe` and so no compiler.
Without the plugin akit serves 6% more than fit; with it, 69% of fit. What the 14.7 µs is made of
(the hand-off to the pool and back, the request copied in, the guest, its host calls) is not
measured apart: that is akio's W5, a performance pass over akio-wasm still open in akio's plan,
asked for with these numbers as **akio API request A180**. Nothing in akit waits on it.

## The nginx converter

fit's sample nginx configs (`adapter/nginx/testdata`: `full.conf`, `proxy.conf`, `static.conf`) and
the other nginx configs the CLI's tests convert (fit's benchmark config, filled in as fitbench fills
it, and its docs' and guides' `nginx-docs.conf` and `nginx-shop.conf`) all convert, and every
directive left out is listed at its line:

| Sample | Directives | Blocks | Warnings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `full.conf` | 38 | 13 | 16 |
| `proxy.conf` | 34 | 7 | 2 |
| `static.conf` | 20 | 9 | 0 |
| `bench-nginx.conf` | 73 | 10 | 35 |
| `nginx-docs.conf` | 19 | 9 | 5 |
| `nginx-shop.conf` | 29 | 8 | 5 |

Blocks count the top level too. What holds it:

- **The same output as fit's, warnings included** (I74): `akit adapt -from nginx` gives fit's JSON
  and fit's warnings byte for byte on 1,290 nginx configs (fit's samples, the assistant's migration
  cases, the benchmark's configs, the docs' configs, the fuzz seeds, every string in the converter's
  tests, and the samples cut short at eleven places), from golden files fit itself wrote; the CLI
  on these samples to JSON, YAML and `.conf`, standard error byte for byte; and every converted
  config loaded or refused by akit's checks as by fit's.
- **Every skipped directive listed** (new in I76,
  `convert::tests::nginx_samples_list_every_skipped_directive`): a directive no converter knows is
  put first in every block of each sample, and at the top, 56 places in all; each time it is reported
  at its own line, or the block it is in is reported skipped as a whole (an `if`, `events`,
  `stream`, a named location), and the converted config is otherwise unchanged. Every warning names
  a line. Checked against a converter changed to drop unknown directives silently, which the test
  fails.

Traefik serves only the proxy; Caddy and Traefik have no cache in fit's harness.

- **First** in fixed (both protocols), static over HTTP/1.1 and all three cache runs, with the
  lowest CPU a request in each of them and the lowest p99 in fixed and static over HTTP/1.1 (0.33
  and 0.45 ms). Within 1% of fit in static over h2c.
- **Third in the proxy**, behind nginx and fit, at 88% and 93% of nginx and with more CPU a request
  (35,683 and 36,281 requests a CPU-second against nginx's 55,066 and 46,002). Its peak memory in
  the proxy, 1.8 GiB, is not akit's own but akio's: the HTTP/1 client's timer thread keeps each
  request's timeout job until the timeout passes, about 240 bytes a request, which I25 found
  (akio API request A171, built on akio's branch `claude/a171-client-timer-cancel`, not yet on
  akio's main or pinned here; with it the proxy's memory stays flat). The cache scenarios go
  through the same client on their misses, which few of their requests are.
- Idle connections: second to nginx over HTTP/1.1 and to fit over TLS; over HTTP/1.1 a little
  more than at the 2026-11-24 baseline (1,136 bytes against 1,068).
